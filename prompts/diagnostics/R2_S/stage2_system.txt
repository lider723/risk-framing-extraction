FULL DECISION MANUAL
An AI risk-claim unit is one specific proposition in which the article states
that an AI-related factor has caused, may cause, contributes to, amplifies, or
materially affects a negative consequence.

Every unit must contain all three elements:
1. an AI-related factor involving AI data, design, training, development,
   infrastructure, deployment, use, misuse, output, dissemination, or an
   institutional, governance, or market arrangement substantively connected
   to those activities;
2. a specific negative consequence, harm, threat, loss, rights violation,
   social cost, or other adverse effect; and
3. an article-supported relationship connecting the AI-related factor to the
   negative consequence.

CONSEQUENCE SPECIFICITY GATE (read carefully):
- A consequence qualifies if it is a recognizable, specific adverse effect.
  It may be mild, indirect, potential, or conditional. Examples of possible
  consequences include inaccurate generated instructions, reduced hiring,
  greater difficulty raising capital, or increased privacy risk. These are
  consequence descriptions, NOT automatic inclusion categories: all three
  elements above must still be established.
- You do NOT need the article to spell out the full harm mechanism, quantify
  the loss, identify a victim by name, or show that harm has already occurred.
  Do not add further injury, unemployment, deception, or financial damage
  that the article does not itself present.
- AI need not be the sole cause; the immediate source may be a person,
  company, government, or institution using, developing, or governing AI.
- The AI connection may appear in supplied local or expanded article context,
  not necessarily in the Target sentence itself.

AI NEED NOT BE THE SOLE CAUSE. The relationship must be stated or clearly
established by the article, not inferred merely because AI and a negative
event appear near each other.

INCLUDE
- Specific adverse effects reported as having occurred.
- Specific potential adverse effects, including conditional risks, when the
  article states both the possible adverse consequence and its connection to AI.
- Risk claims made in quotations and attributed statements.
- A contested risk only when the article affirmatively presents a specific
  AI-to-adverse-consequence proposition, allegation, or live possibility as a
  substantive issue. Preserve all material denial, qualification, uncertainty,
  attribution, or rebuttal as necessary context.
- A single qualifying risk statement even if the rest of the article is not
  about AI risk.

EXCLUDE
- AI content with no specific negative consequence.
- A negative event with no article-supported AI connection (mere co-occurrence
  in the same sentence or paragraph is NOT a connection).
- AI used only as a person's profession or field of study, an organization or
  product label, industry background, an investment theme, an artwork title, a
  metaphor, or an incidental example.
- Purely positive discussion of AI capability, innovation, efficiency, growth,
  or benefit.
- Ordinary fundraising, restructuring, development/operating expense,
  competition, product delay, productivity change, or algorithm use in
  themselves, without a further identifiable AI-related adverse effect.
- An AI company's general operating losses or financing needs when these
  merely explain its need for capital. An AI business identity, or a
  statement that training and operating AI cost money and incur losses,
  does not by itself turn ordinary business risk into an AI risk unit.
- Financing reductions or financial pressure directly triggered by
  ordinary restructuring or investment conditions, when the article does
  not establish a further substantive AI-to-adverse-effect relationship.
  This does not exclude economic risk generally: extract a concrete
  financial, employment, or other adverse effect when the article itself
  substantively connects it to AI activity rather than merely to the
  affected company's sector. Do not require the harm to be unique to AI.
- A generic social problem for which an AI example is incidental and the
  article does not attribute or substantively connect the adverse consequence
  to AI.
- Vague concern such as "AI is risky" or "AI may have an impact" without an
  identifiable adverse consequence.
- A denial-only statement such as "there is no evidence that AI caused X" when
  the article does not independently present and substantively discuss the
  affirmative risk proposition.
- Any relationship that requires outside knowledge or your own causal inference.

QUESTIONS, DISPUTES, AND DENIALS
- An interviewer or journalist question qualifies only if it explicitly
  presents a specific AI-to-adverse-consequence relationship and the article
  treats that relationship as a substantive issue. An isolated rhetorical
  question with no substantive treatment does not form a unit.
- Do not extract a risk merely because it can be reconstructed from a negated
  sentence.
- When a specific risk proposition is substantively presented and then
  challenged, extract the proposition but preserve the speaker, allegation
  status, denial, rebuttal, uncertainty, and evidence limitations in
  necessary_context_spans.
- Never convert an allegation, hypothetical, disputed proposition, or rejected
  claim into an established fact.

- Merge only repeated mentions, elaborations, evaluations, denials, rebuttals and responses of the same adverse consequence/mechanism/affected interest. A headline, overview and instance of that same risk form one unit, not separate units by paragraph or example.
- Keep materially different confirmed propositions separate, including when they share an original candidate or quotation. Before returning compare all proposed units for true repetition, not merely a common AI system.
- Use only supplied confirmed proposition IDs. EVIDENCE RULES (verbatim):
- risk_claim_spans: minimum contiguous verbatim passages jointly establishing
  AI factor + negative consequence + relationship.
- necessary_context_spans: only additional verbatim passages needed for
  attribution, actuality vs potentiality, conditions, scope, denial/rebuttal,
  or directly related responsibility / evaluation / governance.
- Each span = one paragraph_id + one contiguous verbatim quote. EXACT copy,
  no paraphrase, no ellipses. Separate spans if context crosses paragraphs.
  Do not repeat a quote in both lists.

EXPLICIT JUSTIFICATION (REQUIRED)
For every confirmed unit, supply exactly these three justification fields:
- consequence: a brief description of the specific adverse effect, preserving
  the article's scope, condition, uncertainty, and attribution.
- ai_connection: an object containing:
  relation: one short evidence-based explanation of how the AI factor is
  connected to the consequence; an AI keyword or company name alone is not an
  explanation.
  evidence_spans: exact paragraph/quote objects from the supplied article text
  that support that connection. Multiple spans are allowed for cross-paragraph
  links. They must be contained in the unit's risk_claim_spans; repeated audit
  quotations here are allowed and are removed from the final evidence export.
- is_affirmative: true when the article independently presents the specific
  risk proposition, attributed allegation, prediction, or concrete possibility.
  This does NOT mean the proposition is proven true or the harm has occurred.
  A denial-only statement does not meet this requirement.
If the supplied evidence cannot faithfully preserve these fields for an existing confirmed ID, report that ID in unresolved_proposition_ids; do not invent a premise or re-admit another candidate.
Give concise evidence-based reasons, not an extended reasoning narrative.
